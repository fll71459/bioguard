The Message Journey

What happens between pressing Send and seeing an answer? Follow one question through six stations and try each one yourself.

1You ask→ 2Tokens→ 3Context→ 4Predict→ 5Tools→ 6Memory limits
↺ Every answer gets added back into the context, so the loop starts again on your next message.
1
Station 1

You type a question

It all starts with you. You type a message and press Send. To you it is words. To a computer, words are hard to work with. Computers are great with numbers.

So the first job is to turn your words into numbers the AI can read.

2
Station 2

Your words get chopped into tokens

The AI cuts your message into small pieces called tokens. A token is often a whole word, sometimes part of a long word, and sometimes just a comma. Each token has an ID number, like a LEGO piece with a part number.

Characters 0Words 0Tokens 0

This is a classroom version. Real AI models use a "dictionary" of about 100,000 token pieces, but the idea is the same: a short word is usually 1 token, and a long word like "ultrasonic" may become 2 or 3.

Ask the classTry a made-up word like "zorbleflax". Why does it turn into more tokens than "robot"?
3
Station 3

Everything goes into the context window

Your tokens don't go to the AI alone. They are added to the context window: one long page that holds everything the AI is allowed to see right now.

Think of it as a whiteboard. Before every single reply, the AI reads the whole whiteboard from top to bottom. If something isn't on the whiteboard, the AI doesn't know about it.

This is the big surprise: the AI has no memory between messages. It "remembers" your earlier messages only because the app copies the whole conversation back onto the whiteboard every time you press Send.

Ask the classIf you start a brand-new chat, can the AI remember what you told it yesterday? Why or why not?
4
Station 4

The AI guesses the next token, again and again

The AI reads the whole whiteboard and asks one question: "What token most likely comes next?" It picks one, writes it on the whiteboard, then asks again. An answer of 200 words means about 250 guesses in a row, very fast.

It's like the word suggestions on a phone keyboard, but trained on a huge library of text so the guesses are much, much better.

Click a word to choose it yourself, or let the AI pick. The AI usually picks a likely word, but not always the top one. That little bit of chance is why asking the same question twice can give different answers.

Ask the classPress "Start over" and let the AI pick a few times. Did you get the same sentence each time?
5
Station 5

Sometimes it uses a tool

The AI only knows what it learned in training plus what's on the whiteboard. For things like today's weather, a math problem, or a file on your computer, it can ask for a tool. The tool's answer gets written onto the whiteboard, and then the AI keeps guessing tokens.

Step 1You ask"Will it rain at our robot competition on Saturday?"
Step 2AI asks a toolweather("Saturday")
Step 3Result addedThe tool writes 70% rain onto the whiteboard.
Step 4AI answers"Probably! Pack a cover for your robot."
Ask the className a question an AI could not answer without a tool.
6
Station 6

The whiteboard can fill up

The whiteboard has a size limit. Big models can hold hundreds of thousands of tokens, which is a whole book. But when a chat gets too long, the oldest messages get wiped off to make room, and the AI can forget them.

Try it. Pick a whiteboard size, then send the messages one at a time. Watch what happens to Priya's first message.

Recap

Words to remember

Token
A small piece of text (a word or part of one) with an ID number.
Context window
The whiteboard: everything the AI can see right now, read fresh before every reply.
Prediction
The AI writes by guessing the next token, one at a time.
Tool
A helper program (search, calculator, files) whose results get added to the whiteboard.
Context limit
The whiteboard's size. When it's full, old stuff gets wiped and forgotten.

The whole journey in one line: your words become tokens → tokens join everything else on the whiteboard → the AI reads it all and guesses the answer one token at a time → the answer goes on the whiteboard too, ready for your next question.

Made for Robotics for Youth classes. The token numbers and percentages here are simplified for teaching.
